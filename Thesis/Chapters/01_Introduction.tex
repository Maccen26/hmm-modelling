Reduction in the usage of energy in buildings is a crucial aspect
if one wants to go towards a more sustainable future.
It has long been known that buildings are responsible for X\% of the total energy
consumption in the world, and that a large part of this energy
is for HVAC systems [\textcolor{red}{MISSING REFERENCE}].
The energy consumption of HVAC systems has been directly related to the occupant behaviour in the
buildings. This imposes a challenge, as one has to be able to understand the behaviour of the occupants
in the buildings before we can gain the largest optimisation in energy consumption.
(\textcolor{red}{MISSING REFERENCE}) has shown that there is a large variability in the behaviour of occupants, such as
thermal comfort, lighting \& ventilation needs and presence hours.

Within the context of energy consumption in buildings, heating has been estimated
to contribute to X\% [\textcolor{red}{MISSING REFERENCE}] of the consumption.
An aspect of heating of a building is the byproduct of the thermal comfort of the occupants in the building,
and therefore, if we can understand the thermal comfort of the occupants in the buildings,
we can optimise the energy consumption drastically. However, the thermal comfort curve
is a personal preference of the occupants, and we should therefore expect this to differ depending
on the individual buildings and the number of occupants.

This project aims to understand when the occupants of a single office are present,
and how much thermal comfort feedback we need to collect from the occupants in order to be able to
infer the true thermal comfort of the occupants in the building.
The goal is also to build a codebase others can easily download and extend.\\
We will do this by using a Hidden Markov Model (HMM) to model the state of the room.
