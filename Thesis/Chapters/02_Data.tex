\section{Preprocessing of Data}
\subsection{Data Calibration}

When collecting $\text{CO}_2$ data, the sensors tend to drift over time \parencite{pulse2025co2calibration},
and therefore, the data collected from the sensors
are not always accurate. Two identical empty rooms that have the same amount of $\text{CO}_2$ molecyles may show different
readings based on how much the sensors have drifted.
This is a problem, as we want to use the $\text{CO}_2$ data to infer the presence of the occupants in the building, and a
higher baseline of the $\text{CO}_2$ data may lead to a false positive of the presence of the occupants in the building.\\
\\

As a part of this thesis is about predicting occupancy, the calibration algorithms must be constrained to online algorithms.\\
A well known algorithm for calibratig $\text{CO}_2$ data is the ABC (\textit{Automatic Correcting Baseline}) algorithm. This algorithm works by 
find the lowest observed $\text{CO}_2$ readings over some traling window, typically 8 to 14 datys, and subtracting and adding the assumed backround co2 levels. \
However, this algoirhtms rests  on the assumptions that duirng this trailing window, the rooms reaches the baground co2 levels at some point. \cite{senseair_abc}. \\
Looking at Figure~\ref{fig:room_015_raw_floor} of $1.5\%$ quantiles of the raw values over a trailing window of 8 days and 14 days, it is clear to see
that the floor jumps up and down and does not have a clear drift. This is the result of the room not reaching 
the outside $\text{CO}_2$  values of 420 ppm \parencite{icos2023ghg}. Therefore, the ABC algorithm cannot be used directly and have to be modified. 
\input{Plots/data/room_015_raw_floor.tex}
Instead of realying on the assumption that the room will reach the outside CO2 levels during a traling window, it will instead be assumed
that if the change in co2 readings is close to zero, the room has reached a empty room equalibrialiun, where 
the room has been empty for a longer period. 
\bigskip

Let $C_{t}$ be the $\text{CO}_2$ ppm value at time $t$, $\tilde{C}_{t}$ be the corrected $\text{CO}_2$ ppm value at time $t$ and 
$\bar{C}_t$ be the trailing 2 hour mean of $C_t$. $\bar{C}_t$ is used to smooth the ppm values such that a single observation can not give a false 
equlibrium reading. 

A causal anchor baseline $b_t$ will be used, which only uses readings up to time $t$,
such that a calibrated value is never revised. 
The calibrated series is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:calibration_anchor}
\tilde{C}_{t} = C_{t} - b_{t} + 420.
\end{equation}
The baseline starts at $b_0 = 420$ ppm and is updated by two rules:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rule A (down fast):} If $\bar{C}_t < b_t$, the baseline is too high, as the room
    cannot be below fresh air. We then set $b_t = \bar{C}_t$ at once.
    \item \textbf{Rule B (up slowly):} For each day we find the minimum of $\bar{C}_t$. If the slope over the
    3 hours going into the minimum is below 3 ppm/h, the $\text{CO}_2$ has levelled off and the minimum is
    an \textit{anchor}. When the day is over, the baseline ramps linearly towards the anchor over 7 days,
    moving at most 30 ppm per anchor.
\end{itemize}
\input{Plots/data/room_015_anchor_floor.tex}
Since Rule A acts on the smoothed $\bar{C}_t$, the smoothed calibrated series never drops below 420 ppm. The calibrated
30 minute bins are not strictly bounded, since single readings can lie below their 2 hour mean. In Room 015, 81 of the 4827 bins ($1.7\%$)
fall below 420 ppm, spread over 10 days. Most of them lie within 10 ppm of 420 ppm, and the lowest is 388 ppm.
These bins are kept rather than capped at 420 ppm. The ABC algorithm uses the $1.5\%$ quantile rather than the minimum, since a single
erratic reading would otherwise pull the baseline too low, and thereby accepts that about $1.5\%$ of the readings lie below its floor.
The 2 hour mean in Rule A plays the same role, and the $1.7\%$ of bins below 420 ppm are treated as sensor noise that the
emission distribution absorbs, rather than as evidence about the baseline.

\subsection{Data Cleaning}

\subsection{Binned Calibrated CO2 data} \label{sec:binned_calibrated_data}
The calibrated readings are mean aggregated into 30 minute bins. In Room 15 this leaves only 3 empty bins, which form a single
gap during the night of 13 November, as seen in \cref{tab:room_015_gaps}. Interpolating across the gap would use the reading after it
and thereby break the online constraint, so the empty bins are instead forward-filled with the value of the last non-empty bin.
As the $\text{CO}_2$ level barely changes over the gap, from 372 ppm to 369 ppm in the raw readings, the error introduced is small.

\subsection{Temperature Feedback}
The temperature feedback in Room 15 consists of 52 responses from a single user, each choosing one of five answer options.
The feedback is cleaned in two steps. First, 3 exact duplicates are removed. Second, a response is only kept if the same user
has not given a kept response in the same category within the preceding 30 minutes, as repeated clicks on the same option
within a short time express the same complaint. This leaves 38 responses, as seen in \cref{tab:room_015_feedback_counts}.
Most of the removed responses are in Opt 4, where 12 responses are reduced to 3.

\section{Exploratory Data Analysis}
Given that our data now has been calibrated as in \cref{eq:calibration_anchor} and 
binned as descibed in section~\ref{sec:binned_calibrated_data}, we now want to look into some
properties of the cleaning data in Room 15. \\
The first thing we are interested in is whether our data contains autocorrelation.

\input{Plots/data/room_015_calibrated_acf_pacf.tex}
We clearly see in our plot that there is a 24 hour cycle, where the day after is the second most
correlated cycle. We actually see that after 7 days (336 bins), the correlation rises again, and above the day
after the observations. This indicates that there is a high correlation between 7 days, which also makes sense,
as we then can assume that people have the same behaviour on any given Monday.\\
For the partial autocorrelation plot, we see that after 3 bins, there is no more correlation
between the previous observations. We also see that the second bin is negative, which contradicts
the intuition that a previous observation impacts a current observation negatively, but this can also be
understood as the previous observation just carrying less effect into the current observation.
\bigskip

If we look at the whole data series and mark the off days in Figure~\ref{fig:room_015_off_days},
 which are holidays and weekends, we see a clear drop in the $\text{CO}_2$ series on off days, which clearly indicates that
 off days probably do not contain any people in the room.
\input{Plots/data/room_015_off_days.tex}
In \cref{fig:room_015_diurnal_off_days}, we see a clear difference in the pattern of working days and off days, which further
motivates denoting off days and working days. We also see that there is a difference in the $\text{CO}_2$ values when separating
the two types of days over the hours of a day. We see a sudden rise at around 8 and a decline from around 17. This motivates that the working hours
of the offices are 8-17, and that there are people present in those hours.
\input{Plots/data/room_015_diurnal_off_days.tex}

We then check if this is a repeatable behaviour when split out across working days in \cref{fig:room_015_weekly_working_hours}, and
that the general pattern is the same across them. This motivates a covariate denoting the working hours and
also the weekday. There does not seem to be a weekly pattern, such as no people being at the office on Tuesdays.
\input{Plots/data/room_015_weekly_working_hours.tex}

\section{Summary}
In this chapter, the collection method of the data was described, and the calibration technique was presented in
\cref{eq:calibration_anchor}. We have shown strong autocorrelation in the data with a cyclic pattern that peaks every 24 hours. \\
We have seen that the partial autocorrelation is strong for 3 lags and can be interpreted as 1 lag with a less positive value, as in the plot. \\
In the data analytics, we have seen strong indications of a difference in the behaviour of the office between off days and workdays,
and that people are present from 8-17 in Room 15.
