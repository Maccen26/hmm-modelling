\chapter{Data}
The foundation of this thesis is its data. The physical models theorised and the statistical models chosen
are largely determined by the data that has been available in this project. \\
\\
This chapter will present some summary statistics of the dataset used in the thesis and describe how it was collected. % and how it was preprossed \\
Then an office room will be chosen as the focus, and some descriptive analytics will be presented to show properties
of the data and to motivate the physical and statistical models.

\section{Data type \& Collection method}
%Goal in this chapter is to communicate what data is used, how it has been collected,
%how it has been processed,
The data used in this project are $\text{CO}_2$ and feedback from occupants. The $\text{CO}_2$ data comes from sensors that measure $\text{CO}_2$ levels in ppm
and the feedback data is divided into 5 groups, where the occupants could rate the temperature of a office. Both data types was collected from
room 15 in building 303B at DTU (Technical University of Denmark).\\ %(\textit{Matematiktorvet 303B, 2800 Lundtofte}).
Several other office rooms are
also available in the dataset, but room 15 was chosen because it has the most temperature feedback from a single user and exhibits clear signals
of occupancy in the $\text{CO}_2$ readings.\\
The data are provided by
Climify \parencite{climify}, and the data was collected in the period 01-11-2023 to 09-02-2024. There is one sensor in each office,
and the sensors are mounted above the occupied zone.\\
\\
The offices in building 303B belong to employees at DTU, and students do not use these rooms. The rooms are
either offices (for one or more persons) or meeting rooms. \\

\section{Preprocessing of Data}
\subsection{Data Calibration}

When collecting $\text{CO}_2$ data, the sensors tend to drift over time \parencite{pulse2025co2calibration},
and therefore, the data collected from the sensors
is not always accurate. Two identical empty rooms with the same concentration of $\text{CO}_2$ molecules may show different
readings depending on how much the sensors have drifted.
This is a problem, as the $\text{CO}_2$ data is used to infer the presence of occupants in the building, and a
higher baseline in the $\text{CO}_2$ data may lead to a false detection of occupants.\\
\\

As part of this thesis is about predicting occupancy, the calibration algorithm is constrained to be an online algorithm.\\
A well-known algorithm for calibrating $\text{CO}_2$ data is the ABC (\textit{Automatic Baseline Correction}) algorithm [INSERT MISSING REFERENCE]. This algorithm works by
finding the lowest observed $\text{CO}_2$ readings over a trailing window, typically 8 to 14 days, subtracting this floor and adding the assumed background $\text{CO}_2$ level.
However, this algorithm rests on the assumption that the room reaches the background $\text{CO}_2$ level at some point during the trailing window \parencite{senseair_abc}. \\
Looking at the $1.5\%$ quantiles of the raw values over trailing windows of 8 and 14 days in Figure~\ref{fig:room_015_raw_floor}, it is clear
that the floor jumps up and down and does not show a clear drift. This is a result of the room not reaching
the outside $\text{CO}_2$ level of 420 ppm \parencite{icos2023ghg}. Therefore, the ABC algorithm cannot be used directly and has to be modified.
\input{Plots/data/room_015_raw_floor.tex}
Instead of relying on the assumption that the room will reach the outside $\text{CO}_2$ level during a trailing window, it will be assumed
that if the change in the $\text{CO}_2$ readings is close to zero, the room has reached an empty-room equilibrium, where
the room has been empty for a longer period.
\bigskip

Let $C_{t}$ be the $\text{CO}_2$ ppm value at time $t$, $\tilde{C}_{t}$ be the corrected $\text{CO}_2$ ppm value at time $t$ and
$\bar{C}_t$ be the trailing 2 hour mean of $C_t$. $\bar{C}_t$ is used to smooth the ppm values, such that a single observation cannot give a false
equilibrium reading.

A causal anchor baseline $b_t$ will be used, which only uses readings up to time $t$,
such that a calibrated value is never revised.
The calibrated series is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:calibration_anchor}
\tilde{C}_{t} = C_{t} - b_{t} + 420.
\end{equation}
The baseline starts at $b_0 = 420$ ppm and is updated by two rules:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rule A (down fast):} If $\bar{C}_t < b_t$, the baseline is too high, as the room
    cannot be below fresh air. The baseline is then set to $b_t = \bar{C}_t$ at once.
    \item \textbf{Rule B (up slowly):} For each day, the minimum of $\bar{C}_t$ is found. If the slope over the
    3 hours going into the minimum is below 3 ppm/h, the $\text{CO}_2$ has levelled off and the minimum is
    an \textit{anchor}. When the day is over, the baseline ramps linearly towards the anchor over 7 days,
    moving at most 30 ppm per anchor.
\end{itemize}
The resulting baseline for Room 15 is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:room_015_anchor_floor}.
\input{Plots/data/room_015_anchor_floor.tex}
Since Rule A acts on the smoothed $\bar{C}_t$, the smoothed calibrated series never drops below 420 ppm. The calibrated
30 minute bins are not strictly bounded, since single readings can lie below their 2 hour mean. In Room 15, 81 of the 4827 bins ($1.7\%$)
fall below 420 ppm, spread over 10 days. Most of them lie within 10 ppm of 420 ppm, and the lowest is 388 ppm.
These bins are kept rather than capped at 420 ppm. The ABC algorithm uses the $1.5\%$ quantile rather than the minimum, since a single
erratic reading would otherwise pull the baseline too low, and thereby accepts that about $1.5\%$ of the readings lie below its floor.
The 2 hour mean in Rule A plays the same role, and the $1.7\%$ of bins below 420 ppm are treated as sensor noise that the
emission distribution absorbs, rather than as evidence about the baseline.

\subsection{Binned Calibrated \texorpdfstring{$\text{CO}_2$}{CO2} Data} \label{sec:binned_calibrated_data}
The calibrated readings are mean aggregated into 30 minute bins. In Room 15 this leaves only 3 empty bins, which form a single
gap during the night of 13 November, as seen in \cref{tab:room_015_gaps}. Interpolating across the gap would use the reading after it
and thereby break the online constraint, so the empty bins are instead forward-filled with the value of the last non-empty bin.
As the calibrated $\text{CO}_2$ level only changes from 407.7 ppm to 413.5 ppm over the gap, the error introduced is small.

\subsection{Temperature Feedback}
The temperature feedback in Room 15 consists of 52 responses from a single user, each choosing one of five answer options.
The feedback is cleaned in two steps. First, 3 exact duplicates are removed. Second, a response is only kept if the same user
has not given a kept response in the same category within the preceding 30 minutes, as repeated clicks on the same option
within a short time express the same complaint. This leaves 38 responses, as seen in \cref{tab:room_015_feedback_counts}.
Most of the removed responses are in Opt 4, where 12 responses are reduced to 3.

\section{Exploratory Data Analysis}
Given that the data has now been calibrated as in \cref{eq:calibration_anchor} and
binned as described in Section~\ref{sec:binned_calibrated_data}, some properties of the cleaned data in Room 15
are now examined.

\subsection{Autocorrelation}
The first question is whether the data contains autocorrelation.

\input{Plots/data/room_015_calibrated_acf_pacf.tex}
Figure~\ref{fig:room_015_calibrated_acf_pacf} clearly shows a 24 hour cycle, where the lag of one day is the most
correlated of the daily peaks. After 7 days (336 bins), the correlation rises again, to a level above the lag of one day.
This indicates a high correlation between observations 7 days apart, which also makes sense,
as people can then be assumed to behave the same way on any given Monday.\\
The partial autocorrelation shows no more correlation with the previous observations after 3 bins.
The partial autocorrelation at the second bin is also negative. This may seem
counterintuitive, as it suggests that a previous observation impacts the current observation negatively, but it can also be
understood as the previous observation simply carrying less effect into the current observation.

\subsection{Working Days and Working Hours}
Figure~\ref{fig:room_015_off_days} shows the whole data series with the off days, which are holidays and weekends, marked.
There is a clear drop in the $\text{CO}_2$ series on off days, which indicates that
the room is most likely empty on off days.
\input{Plots/data/room_015_off_days.tex}
Figure~\ref{fig:room_015_diurnal_off_days} shows a clear difference between the patterns of working days and off days, which further
motivates distinguishing between off days and working days. There is also a difference in the $\text{CO}_2$ values over the hours of the day
when separating the two types of days. There is a sudden rise at around 8 and a decline from around 17. This suggests that the working hours
of the office are 8--17, and that people are present during those hours.
\input{Plots/data/room_015_diurnal_off_days.tex}

Figure~\ref{fig:room_015_weekly_working_hours} shows whether this behaviour repeats across the individual working days, and
the general pattern is the same across them. This motivates a covariate denoting the working hours and
also the weekday. There does not seem to be a weekly pattern, such as no people being at the office on Tuesdays.

\section{Summary}
In this chapter, the data used in the thesis was presented. The data consists of $\text{CO}_2$ readings from room 15 in building 303B at DTU,
collected by Climify in the period 01-11-2023 to 09-02-2024, together with temperature feedback from a single user.\\
\\
Because the sensors drift and the room does not reach the outside level of 420 ppm, the ABC algorithm could not be used directly.
Instead, an online calibration with a causal anchor baseline was presented in \cref{eq:calibration_anchor}. The baseline moves down at once
when the smoothed readings fall below it, and moves up slowly towards daily anchors where the $\text{CO}_2$ has levelled off.
The calibrated readings were mean aggregated into 30 minute bins, where a single gap of 3 bins was forward-filled to keep the online constraint,
and the temperature feedback was cleaned from 52 to 38 responses.\\
\\
The exploratory data analysis showed strong autocorrelation in the data, with a cyclic pattern that peaks every 24 hours
and a correlation at a lag of 7 days above the lag of one day. The partial autocorrelation is significant for the first 3 lags,
where the negative second lag can be interpreted as the earlier observation carrying less effect into the current observation. \\
Finally, there were strong indications of a difference in the behaviour in the office between off days and working days,
and that people are present from 8--17 in Room 15. This pattern is the same across the working days, which motivates covariates
for the working hours and the weekday in the models.
